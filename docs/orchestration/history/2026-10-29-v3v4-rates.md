### They do **not** share one cause, and only one of them is mine to fix

The brief asked the question first, and the measurement answers it in two
different places.

**V4 is the test's.** The two reds are at **`follower_alive.rs:199`**, not at
the `:231` the register names:

```
panicked at herd-hub/tests/follower_alive.rs:199:5:
assertion `left == right` failed: [...]
  left: 9
 right: 8
```

Nine liveness frames where the window wanted eight. The loop exits on the
eighth breath; `breathed_at`, `spread` and `longest` were then computed over
**everything heard**, which includes the stretch after the gate opens — the
`sh` loop waking to find the gate, the word it prints, the verdict. A ninth
breath lands in that tail whenever finishing takes longer than one cadence,
which under contention it does. `03b §9` had already read this defect off
the source and marked it F; the measurement is what was missing, and it puts
the failure one assertion earlier than the register says.

**V3 is the product's**, and that is the brief's stop condition. Below.

### V4, fixed: the window is the one the gate holds shut

`herd-hub/tests/follower_alive.rs`, one function, +65/−5:

- **`let during_the_silence = heard.len();`** at the loop's exit. Every
  figure is taken over `heard[..during_the_silence]`. The silence this test
  is about is the one the gate holds shut, so that is the window — decided
  by the test, not by the scheduler.
- **`assert!(longest < spread / 2)` is gone.** It was a bound on one measured
  duration taken from another measured duration, and it was not a statement
  about the hub: it passed or failed on how long the tail happened to be. The
  upper bound on silence is the absolute `allowed` — ten cadences — which is
  what a proxy's idle timeout is: a figure, not a fraction of however long
  this run took. `03c` item 12 reached the same verdict ("Proved") and `03b`
  recommended exactly this scoping.
- **What the ratio was really guarding is kept, in the safe direction.** Its
  content is "most of the breaths bunched, one far away", and that is a gap
  too **short**, not a gap too long. So the gaps between breaths are
  asserted against the cadence the hub was given. The **median** of the seven
  and not the minimum, for a measured reason: the hub's ticker catches up
  after a tick it missed, so a frame held up by contention shortens the gap
  *after* it, and the shortest gap of a run reached **145.7 ms** in 1 of 40
  loaded runs against a floor of 133 ms. A bound on the minimum would be a
  bound contention can reach, which is the mistake this whole change is
  about. The median of seven moves only if four of them do.

Nothing about what the test is for changed: the placement order, the spread
bound, the absolute silence bound and the verdict assertion are all as they
were, and `BREATH`, `BREATHS_WANTED` and `BREATH_EVERY` are untouched.

### V3: the cause is in the product, so it stops here

`a_runner_killed_mid_job_gives_its_slot_back` did not fail once in 40 runs.
It is not stable — it is *not reproducible by load on this host*, and the
reason is that the regime it is fragile against needs its own runner
descheduled for **two whole seconds**, which twelve cores do not do even at
load1 13.6 on two pinned ones. So it was driven on purpose.

**The timeline, measured** (instrumented copy, hub and runner journals; the
~10 s before the first line is the shared in-test `cargo build`):

```
+10.077  the runner is killed with SIGKILL
+10.098  the replacement runner has joined
+12.070  the torn-out job has a second attempt   (= the kill + 1.993 s)
+12.0696 the test's guard says a runner is connected
+12.0882 the third build is submitted
```

The hub's own sentence for the write-off names the threshold:

```
herd-hub: j0000000002 is queued again for attempt 1 of 2 at a runner that
went away: runner wsl-pc is connected and the health report it sent
164.058787ms ago does not name attempt 1 of this job, and nothing has been
heard about that attempt for 2.000310654s: the attempt is gone from a
machine that is still here
```

So the test's premise is built at `--lost-after` to the millisecond, and the
window between its guard and its submission is **19 ms**. The comment at
`slots.rs:266-276` already says what goes wrong in that window.

**Forced, five runs each side of the threshold.** The replacement runner is
stopped with `SIGSTOP` for N ms after the guard passes, then continued, then
the third build is submitted — which is what a two-core box does to a healthy
runner, done on purpose:

| Starvation | vs `--lost-after` 2000 ms | Runs | Green | Red |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2200 ms | over | 5 | 0 | **5** |
| 1500 ms | under | 5 | **5** | 0 |

The red is one sentence, five times:

```
the slot is held by the job that was given another go: queued 1st of 1 at
priority step, rising to merger in 30m; it is waiting because no runner is
connected to this hub; nothing is ahead of it
```

**And no test-side change can hold the premise against it.** I waited on the
fact instead of snapshotting it — 60 polls at 250 ms — and it never becomes
true: within 250 ms of the starvation the *doomed* job reads `finished`, its
second of two goes written off by the same threshold, so the slot is freed,
the third build is placed and its `waiting_for` disappears altogether. The
hub took the honest decision at every step; what destroyed the premise is
that **one threshold does two jobs** — it writes off the attempt of a machine
that is gone, and it drops the link of a machine that is merely late — and a
test that needs the first to be short needs the second to be long.

That is `herd-hub/src/server.rs:5597` (the link check) against `:5619` (the
attempt check), both reading `policy.lost_after`. `03b §9` read it the same
way and recommended **not** fixing it by raising the threshold until the link
clock has an answer like the attempt clock's (D169). So: no edit, a finding, a
board row and the question below.

## Risks and findings

1. **Four sightings in one day of one shape: the code had the answer and
   threw it away.** V4 held the index where the silence ended and recomputed
   its figures over a window it had not bounded; V3 holds the hub's own
   sentence about the slot and reads a snapshot of a wait reason instead;
   `sweep.rs` turned an `io::Error` into a boolean at four sites; D208's test
   waited on a frame the hub had *sent* rather than one the runner had
   *read*. This is what the measurement here is **of**, and it is one named
   finding for the landing record rather than four notes. Naming why a timing
   is being consulted at all is what stops the next test being written this
   way — a rate only tells us how often the timings lie.
2. **The register's line number for V4 is one assertion late.** `:230`/`:231`
   is what ORCH-022 and `00-baseline.md §8` name, and it is a real defect of
   the same kind, but on this host what goes red is `:199`. Anyone matching a
   CI failure against the row should expect either.
3. **The new floor's margin is 14%, and it is one-sided.** 133 ms against a
   cadence the hub fires at 154 ms. Contention can only lengthen a gap; what
   can shorten one is the ticker's catch-up after a missed tick, and that is
   why the median is asserted and not the minimum. If a future change makes
   the hub fire *late* rather than early, `floor` and `least` both want
   re-reading — they share the `BREATH * 2 / 3` figure and its argument.
4. **`allowed` is now the only upper bound on silence, and it is loose**: ten
   cadences, 11.3× the worst gap measured under load. That is deliberate — it
   is the proxy-idle-timeout statement and it must survive a CI stall — but
   it means a hub that went quiet for nine cadences once would pass. Tightening
   it is a figure to argue with evidence, not a ratio to re-derive; the
   distribution above is the evidence a future task would start from.
5. **The same window defect may be elsewhere.** The sibling test in this file
   (`a_queued_job_whose_reason_is_not_restated_is_kept_warm_anyway`) counts
   breaths and asserts nothing about timing, so it is clean. I did not survey
   the other test files; `grep -rn "windows(2)" herd-hub/tests/` and
   `grep -rln "Instant::now" herd-hub/tests/` are the survey, and it is worth
   a board row rather than an assumption.
6. **V3 will still go red on CI**, and when it does it is the product's link
   clock talking, not a flake. The sentence to look for is `no runner is
   connected to this hub` in a `waiting_for`, or `dropping the connection to
   runner wsl-pc: nothing has come back on it for ~2s` on the hub's stderr.
7. **Nothing in CI was re-run**, no push to `main`, no PR, no tag. PID
   3046930, the stray `herd-hub` on `127.0.0.1:18461`, was left alone and its
   port never taken.

## Decisions needed

**One, and it is the product's.** Should `--lost-after` keep doing two jobs —
deciding that a machine which is **gone** has lost its attempt, and that a
machine which is merely **late** has lost its link?

- **Recommendation:** separate them, as D169 separated the attempt clock from
  the machine's own word. The link check at `server.rs:5597` is the one that
  drops a healthy runner, and it is the half with no answer from the machine;
  D169's shape applies to it directly — a connection that has reported
  *recently enough to be answering* is not a connection to drop, however
  stale the last report's arithmetic looks on a reactor thread the hub's own
  dispatch can monopolise. Until that exists, `slots.rs:206` stays on the
  register as the evidence, which is `03b §9`'s recommendation and not mine.
- **Fallback if that is too big:** a second threshold for the link alone,
  defaulting to a multiple of `--lost-after`, so a test can keep the attempt
  clock short without making a late runner a dead one. Cheaper, and it leaves
  the 12:14 incident's mechanism in place.
- **What I did instead:** nothing. No product edit, no widened threshold.
