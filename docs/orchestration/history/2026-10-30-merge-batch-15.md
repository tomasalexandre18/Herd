## What changed and why

**M11's pack half stops being wired only at the edges.** Before this
branch the hub's loop could not reach its pack registry at all: `State`
holds an `Arc<dyn JobStore>` while `packs()` was an inherent method on
the concrete `JobDirStore`, so `runner_gone` and `forget_all_holdings`
had no reachable call site, and **all six pack `ToHub` frames fell
through to `other =>` and were answered `FromHub::Refused`, which closes
the link.** A runner that announced a holding therefore lost its
connection. That is the defect this landing closes.

What `main` can do now that it could not before: a hub **told** about a
pack holding records it, with the mesh endpoint the runner's handshake
advertised, and **forgets it when the link goes** — on both of the hub's
"a runner is gone" paths, the closed link and the watchdog. Six frames
are dispatched behind two named arrival-window predicates, and a frame
outside its own window is refused **by name** as `FromHub::PackRefused`
rather than `FromHub::Refused`, so a pack nobody can agree about no
longer costs a runner its link.

**What is still owed is stated as owed, and the tree says so itself.**
D231 decision 5 is **not** closed by this landing, on **both sides of
the ask**: the hub does not send `FromHub::ReadPacksAgain` and a runner
neither answers an ask nor announces unprompted, so an announcement only
ever arrives unprompted and today's runner sends none. Every measurement
of the holdings mechanism is **against a hand-driven peer, and nothing
is measured against the real runner binary**. `herd-hub/src/registry.rs`'s
module note now says exactly that, replacing the note that said none of
it was reachable. The record does not improve on it.

## Risks and findings

1. **This landing's claim is narrow on purpose.** A hub told about a
   holding records and forgets it; nothing says a real runner ever tells
   one. The temptation at integration is to write "the pack frames are
   wired" and stop. The tree does not overclaim — `registry.rs`'s module
   note states the gap — and neither does this record.
2. **Two mergers shared this host.** Batch 14 was gating
   `runner-silence` while this batch was prepared. The gate was not
   started while that suite was running, because the contention class
   ORCH-107 names is the one thing that turns a green branch red, and
   because `workflow.md` caps this host at two suites and this agent was
   told to wait rather than race. The margin the run started on is in
   the report, stated as a margin and **never quoted as a bar**.
   **It cost an id collision**, caught by reading the host rather than
   the origin: *Decisions allocated* 3. Nothing in the method would
   have caught it — `check-docs.sh` checks a row's eight cells and its
   state word and **not** whether two branches claim one `ORCH-nnn`,
   and a branch listing shows only what is on the origin, never an
   unlanded integration branch in another worktree. The board is the
   one list of open work (invariant 8) and two mergers allocating from
   it at once have nothing between them but this reading.
   **The wait was the right call and it was paid for twice.** Batch
   14's gate went green and it landed first, so `main` moved under
   this batch and the integration branch had to be rebuilt on its tip
   — one board conflict, resolved above. Racing would have replaced
   that conflict with two suites contending on one twelve-core host,
   and a red step from contention is indistinguishable from a red step
   from the change. A rebuild is cheap and attributable; a contended
   suite is neither.
3. **`docs/status.md` was at its 90-line cap exactly**, so the pack
   paragraph and the *does not do* item were reflowed to the same total
   rather than grown; the file is 90 lines. Two details moved out of
   status into D247 and the board — the two store guards' glosses are
   shortened — which is what a state file that points elsewhere is for.
4. **`bc` is not on this host's PATH, and a wait loop that uses it
   waits its whole cap.** Observed read-only while deciding whether to
   race: batch 14's pre-gate wait printed
   `bc: command not found` on every iteration, so its
   `[ "$(echo "$l < 5.0" | bc -l)" = 1 ]` test was false at every
   reading and its counter never advanced — it waited the full 25
   minutes at a one-minute load of about 2.0 to 2.9, which its own
   condition would have cleared within a minute. Nothing was touched
   and this is not a defect of that landing; it is a host fact worth a
   gotcha, and it is the same family as the semicolon and the pipe: a
   shell construct that **fails open and prints its failure where
   nobody is reading**. `awk "BEGIN{exit !(...)}"` is what
   `scripts/gate.sh` itself uses for exactly this comparison.
5. **The author's own gotcha, carried forward.** A pipe discards an
   exit status exactly as `;` does, so every phase of this gate is
   joined with `&&` and no gate output is read through `tail` or
   `head` in a way that could eat a status. The gotcha sheet still
   does not mention
   the pipe; that amendment is unlanded and named under *Left out*.
