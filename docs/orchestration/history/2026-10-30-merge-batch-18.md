## What changed and why

**M11's pack half stops being wired at the edges: a job is now assigned
its packs** (E-12, E-17, ORCH-234, ORCH-239). The premise on ORCH-234's
row was one step short, and the branch said so: `Asked.needs` and
`Asked.leaves` *did* ride the wire and were **dropped at `new_job`**,
reaching no `NewJob`, no `JobRecord` and no `Declaration` — so there was
nothing at the assignment site to resolve. They are now record fields
carried by a new `NewJob::consuming`, resolved against the registry at
the assignment and **before `begin_attempt`**, so a job that waits for a
pack has not spent one of its goes on a machine that never saw it and no
record names a runner handed nothing. **An unresolvable key is a wait,
named, and never a `Refused`** (D13), in four distinct cases. The runner
stops discarding the two fields and **hands a job back with every key
named** rather than running it: `{in}/<key>` would have been an empty
directory and `{out}` swept with nothing registered, so the job would
have exited 0 and looked green while the pack a later job waits for
never came to exist — D150's silence.

**A detached job's end is announced when its submitter asked** (E-37,
M13, **D250**). One `bool`, `announce_end`, on `ToHub::SubmitJob`, on
`NewJob` and on `job.json`, asked for by `herdctl submit --announce-end`
and by nothing else. The gate on emission is that field **on the
record** and not whether anybody is following: a follower list is a fact
about one second, and E-37 asks what the submitter wanted at submission
time. The event fires at **every** door a record can reach a terminal
state through, the green ones included.

**What `main` can do now that it could not before:** place a job against
a pack registry and tell a requester, by name, which key it is waiting
for; and tell a submitter who has gone away how their job ended.
