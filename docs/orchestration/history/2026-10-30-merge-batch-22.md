## What changed and why

**One product change, two rules written down, and a batch with no
conflict at all.** This moves no milestone and touches no `E-nn`
expectation: it is the method and the runner's confinement, not a
feature. `docs/coverage.md` is untouched and the musts tally stays at 42
of 44 (D248).

`main` can now do two things it could not. The runner **reads** an
`EBUSY` from `cgroup.subtree_control` instead of guessing at it: a
process standing directly in the delegated base is identified through
`cgroup.procs` and `/proc`, this runner's own children are moved into
its leaf and one retry follows, while a process that is nobody's of this
runner's becomes a refusal **naming its pid** rather than a silent fall
back to `RLIMIT_AS` — a per-process address-space limit where a job-tree
ceiling was promised. Only `EBUSY` takes that path; `EACCES` stays a
permission and `ENOENT` a missing controller. And the project's own
execution rule is in `CLAUDE.md` *Commands*, black on white as Alexa
asked twice on 2026-10-30: every build, suite and gate goes to our own
Herd instance through the wrapper, with `--label build` and
`--memory-mib`, after `bench-preflight.sh`; a failed job is reported and
never re-run locally for a greener answer; a local run is permissible
only as a **named control**.

What it still cannot do: **bound anything by cgroup on this host.** Our
runner has no delegated cgroup, so Herd orders work and does not bound
it, and the twelve delegation assertions are still unreachable without a
user scope — which, as D260 now requires, must carry its own
`MemoryMax` or it silences `bench-preflight.sh`'s own guard. Whether a
runner should **refuse to start** when its base is occupied is not
settled here: it is **ORCH-272**, and it is Alexa's.
