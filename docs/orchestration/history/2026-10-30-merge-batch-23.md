## What changed and why

This batch moves **M12**, the pack milestone, on its two open fronts,
and the two branches touch no product file in common.

**E-07's promise stops being half a promise.** `would-run` answered
about the claim step and nothing after it, and a job with `needs`
resolves its keys **after** the claim — `packs_for` runs inside
`dispatch`. So a job the fleet fits and the registry cannot serve was
claimed, held back by name, left queued, and the dry run said *"it
would start now"* about it. That was not a corner case: nothing under
`herd-runner/src` constructs `ToHub::RegisterPack` (ORCH-260), so
**every** job with `needs` was in exactly that state and the dry run was
wrong about all of them. `would-run` now consults `packs_for` for the
one job it is about, read-only, and answers *"it would be claimed and
then wait for its packs"*, naming the key and the commit (**D261**).

**The pack budget is spent for the first time.** `evict_to_budget`
landed in batch 21 with no caller at all (ORCH-264): written, tested
against a fixture, and never run. It now has one —
`Session::sweep_packs_to_budget`, once per connection, in `serve_once`,
**before** the announcement of what the runner holds, with the hub's
`keeper_for`/`PinPacks` set in force (**D262**). Asserted against the
real binary as two processes on two identical disks under one budget.

What `main` can do now that it could not before: answer a dry run for a
job that needs packs without lying about it, and come under a pack
budget on a runner that redials. What it still cannot do is **keep** a
pack on purpose in the field, because no hub names a keeper yet.

**The gate runs whole, not one job per step, and the reason is
mechanical.** The instruction was to prefer one job per gate *if a
receipt for the gated sha can still be produced that way*. It cannot:
`scripts/gate.sh` writes the receipt in its own **step 8**, after step 7
has judged the skip lists of the suite step 6 ran, so a set of
per-step jobs produces no receipt at all and `pre-push` would refuse the
push. So it runs whole, with
`CARGO_TARGET_DIR=/mnt/ssd_980/herd/build/batch-23` exported inside the
job before the gate — kept for the root filesystem's sake, and **not**
as protection against the runner's disk cap, which is the claim the
paragraphs below retire.

**What `bytes_written` is, measured 2026-10-30 with a control, because
this landing was briefed on a theory of it that is now refuted.** It is
**not** the bytes a job writes. It is a recursive walk of the job's
workspace base — `<data-root>/jobs/<job-id>/`, so `work/`, `home/`,
`tmp/`, `in/` and `out/` — summing **apparent** size, run every 200 ms,
reported as `max(peak, final)`. Two consequences follow, and both are
the kind of thing that gets re-derived wrongly later:

1. **The cap can kill a job that wrote nothing.** `truncate -s 8G` in
   the workspace records **8 GiB** against **16,384** real bytes.
2. **The cap is void for every ad-hoc job** the moment it `cd`s out of
   its workspace, so it binds only a *declared* command. Not this
   landing's to fix, and written down so that nobody reads this
   project's green gates as evidence that the bound works.

**So batch 22's death was a sampling artefact and not a tree.** The
suite's own fixtures are what trip it: `packstore.rs`'s `plant_pack`
creates **sparse** pack objects with `File::set_len` at 1, 2, 4, 6 and
15 GiB, and `testing::TempDir` puts them under `$TMPDIR`, which is
**inside** the measured base. A 0.5 s sampler caught that base at
**21,564,710,785 apparent bytes against 109,563,904 real** — 20 GiB of
holes, about 104 MiB of actual disk — in a window lasting **0.11 s**.
Batch 22's poll landed inside that window and its job died
(`cause stopped_by_cap`, `cap disk`, `limit 21474836480`,
`bytes_written 22636083365`, no verdict, no receipt); another job doing
the same work missed the window and recorded 89 MB. **The 250x spread
measures whether a poll landed, not the work.**

**And the redirect is cap-neutral, measured.** 300 MiB written into the
worktree records `0`, and 300 MiB written into `$HERD_BUILD_ROOT`
records `0` as well — both paths are equally unmeasured, so neither
splitting the gate nor redirecting its target directory ever touched the
measured path at all. Batch 21's single undivided `gate.sh` recorded
8.08 GiB **with** the redirect. This record therefore does **not** claim
that the redirect protects the job, and a later reader should not
restore that claim. The measurement and its control live on branch
`bytes-written-measured` at `d9795b9`, prose only, **held for the next
batch and deliberately not merged here** so that this integration did
not grow a fourth merge after it was ready to gate.

**A capped job is a block and not a red gate.** It produces no verdict,
so it cannot be reported as one, it says **nothing** about the branches
in this batch, and nothing is re-run locally for a greener answer. If
the gate job is capped it is resubmitted **once** through the queue and
**both** jobs' `bytes_written` go in the merger's report. A local run
would be named as a control if one happened. **Every job this landing
submits reports its `bytes_written`**, now as a figure whose meaning is
known rather than as evidence about a redirect.
