## What changed and why

**M11's layout step, which is the batch's one product change, and two
measurements that cost no production code.** This moves M11 and it
touches E-12 and E-17; E-42's own recount is deliberately not here.

`herdctl` read `needs` and `leaves` off `commands.toml`, validated them
and threw them away, and nothing under `herd-runner/src` ever
constructed `ToHub::RegisterPack` — two breaks in one chain, ORCH-274
and ORCH-260. Both halves land together, in one branch, because filling
the fields alone would make every declared command that names `needs`
wait at a dispatch where nothing registers a pack (**D267**).

**What `main` can do now that it could not before:** a declared command
leaves a pack, the hub registers it on `(commit, key)`, a later declared
command at that commit is laid out with it under `{in}`, and its own
tool reads the bytes — proved against the real hub, runner and `herdctl`
as processes in `herd-hub/tests/pack_chain.rs`.

**What it still cannot do, and the record says so rather than implying
otherwise:** the transfer in that proof is `SameHost` and **nought bytes
off the network**. The hub mints no transfer capability —
`ToHub::AskTransferCapability` is refused by name,
`FromHub::ExpectTransfer` is sent by nothing, and
`endpoint::Granted::pushed_by_hub` has no production feeder — so **a
pack cannot move between two machines at all**, which is E-12's whole
point. That is **ORCH-286**, filed by this landing because no row named
it, and it is now the critical path. The runner's side of the round
trip is complete, so the peer path starts working the day the hub mints,
with no runner change.
