### Part one — the seam (commit 1)

`JobStore::packs()` answers `Option<&crate::registry::PackRegistry>`
with a **default body of `None`**, in `herd-hub/src/job.rs` at the end of
the trait. `herd-hub/src/store.rs`'s `impl JobStore for JobDirStore`
overrides it with `Some(JobDirStore::packs(self))` and is the only
implementor that does.

**What `None` means, in the method's own doc:** *"this store keeps no
pack registry", and never "the registry it keeps is empty"*. The doc
spells out the consequence, because the two are a fork in the hub's
behaviour and not a shade of the same thing: a store answering `None`
can register nothing, resolve nothing and hold nothing, so every pack
frame such a hub is sent is refused **by name** saying so; a store
answering `Some` with an empty registry is a working authority that has
been told about no pack, and its frames are answered on their merits.
Telling a runner "nothing is registered for that key" from a hub that
could never have registered anything is D150's silence in a second
place — true of the key, false about why.

**Why the default body and not a field on `State`:** no implementor and
no test double had to move. Four of the five `impl JobStore` in the tree
are doubles with no state root (`server.rs`'s
`AStoreWithAWindowForcedOpen`, `tests/cost_surface.rs`,
`tests/junction_breakdown.rs`, `tests/unsynced_write_cost.rs`); a field
on `State` would have grown an argument at every construction site of
it. This is ORCH-230's open question and the recommendation the brief
carried; it is answered as recommended.

Proved by
`a_store_behind_the_trait_object_reaches_the_very_registry_its_root_keeps`
in `herd-hub/tests/pack_registry.rs`: the registry comes back **through
an `Arc<dyn JobStore>`**, which is the only form the hub ever holds a
store in; it answers `Some` while still empty; it is the registry of
that root and not a second one opened on the side; and a second look
through the trait object sees what the first one wrote.

### Part two — the frames (commit 2)

Six pack `ToHub` frames dispatched in `relay_from_runner`. Before this,
**all six** fell through to the `other =>` arm and were answered
`FromHub::Refused`, which closes the link (D13): a runner that announced
what it holds lost its connection for it.

| frame | answered with | arrival window |
|---|---|---|
| `PacksHeld` | journal line; no wire answer exists | the registry's presence |
| `RegisterPack` | `PackRegistered` / `PackRefused` | `a_pack_may_be_registered` |
| `AskPack` | `PackHolders` / `PackRefused` | `a_pack_may_be_fetched` |
| `PackUnavailable` | journal line | — (a job fact) |
| `PackTransferred` | journal line | — (figures only) |
| `AskTransferCapability` | `PackRefused`, non-terminal | not implemented |

**The two arrival-window predicates, which is the part of this the
brief said to write well if nothing else.** Both are in `server.rs`
beside `files_may_land`, which is the predicate D150 paid for and which
they are modelled on.

`a_pack_may_be_fetched(state, from, job_id) -> Result<u32, String>` —
the window of a **fetch**. Inside: `assigned`, `running` or
`interrupted`, on the attempt the record names, from the runner the
record names. Outside, each refused by name: a job that does not exist;
a job that is **over**, whose `{in}` nothing will ever read; a job the
hub has taken back to `queued`, which is the case most worth naming
because the asking runner is still preparing a job that will run
somewhere else; a job with no attempt; another runner's attempt.

`a_pack_may_be_registered(state, from, job_id, attempt)` — the window of
a **registration**, at the opposite end of a job's life. Inside: the
attempt the frame names is the **last** attempt on the record and ran on
this runner, **whether or not the job is over** — which is the whole
point, because the runner fsyncs the pack out of `{out}` before the
record walk (D232-D234) and the frame therefore routinely arrives after
the verdict, which `is_current` refuses by name as
`Standing::JobIsOver`. Outside: no such job; an unreadable record; no
attempt; an attempt that is not the last one; another runner's attempt.

`a_pack_registry(state)` is the third guard and the one that makes
`None` mean what its doc says: it answers the registry or the one
sentence that says this hub keeps none.

**`runner_gone` is wired on both paths**, and the watchdog's ordering is
untouched. `release_runner`'s site, in `serve_a_runner`, is the closed-link
path; the watchdog's is inside the `for (name, quiet_for) in silent_links`
loop that already runs **after** the `with_peers` closure that removes
the runner and sends its kick — so it is outside the peers mutex and
after the existing closure, which is the only shape ORCH-230 permits.
Nothing in that pass was reordered (D228, ORCH-022).

**`FromHub::ReadPacksAgain` is NOT sent, and that is this branch's one
withdrawal.** It was written where it belongs — to every runner
immediately after `Welcome`, since the hub cannot tell its own restart
from a runner's first join — and the suite went red: `13 passed; 6
failed` in `herd-hub/tests/link.rs` and `3 passed; 1 failed` in
`herd-hub/tests/versions.rs`, all seven reading the frame *after* the
welcome and finding this one. Six of the seven are about admission,
cadence, twins and verdicts and have nothing to do with packs.

The seven are brittle rather than right: a real runner ignores a frame
it has no handler for, which is what D148 bought. So the fix is to make
those helpers skip frames they are not about — **seven loosened
expectations in two test files this task was not pointed at**, and
loosening an expectation to turn a gate green is the one thing an
execution agent may not do on its own account. The product change was
removed instead, the test that asserted it was removed with it rather
than weakened, and the measurement is written at that spot in
`server.rs`, in `registry.rs`'s module note, and under *Decisions
needed* below.

**One field the hub was discarding.** `ToHub::Hello`'s `mesh_endpoint`
was bound and dropped (`mesh_endpoint: _`). It is now kept on
`Announced` and on `ConnectedRunner`, read by `State::mesh_endpoint_of`,
and passed to `PackRegistry::announced`. Without it every holding the
hub recorded would have come back with `endpoint: None`, which on the
wire means **outbound-only** — a false statement about a runner that
advertised an address, and one a fetcher would have acted on.

### What is proved, and how

`herd-hub/tests/pack_frames.rs`, five tests, all through a socket
against a real hub and a hand-driven peer. The file calls the registry
**not once**: every assertion is about what a hub answered on a link,
because what was missing was never the registry.

- `an_announcement_replaces_what_the_hub_held_and_an_empty_one_retracts_it`
  — the replace rule, `more: true` accumulating without applying, the
  endpoint, and the **empty announcement as a retraction**, all asserted
  by asking the hub through `AskPack` rather than by reading its memory
- a_pack_is_registered_after_the_verdict_and_a_wrong_attempt_is_refused_by_name
- a_fetch_for_a_job_that_is_over_is_refused_by_name_and_not_answered_with_a_key
  — in-window first, then out, so the test distinguishes "refused
  because of the window" from "refused always"
- `a_dropped_link_forgets_what_a_runner_held_and_the_rejoin_says_it_again`
- `the_hub_registers_a_pack_and_holds_not_one_byte_of_its_objects`

One fixture carries both windows, deliberately: the same job read before
and after its `JobFinished` is inside both windows at one end and
outside the fetch window while still inside the registration window at
the other. Two jobs could not have shown that the two windows are
different windows.

Every test that provokes a refusal then sends a health report and reads
`HealthRecorded`, so "the link is still up" is measured and not assumed.

**The zero payload bytes, counted.** The fixture manifest declares
**12288** bytes of objects in two `kind f` rows. After a registration
driven through a frame, the hub's **whole state root** — not just
`packs/` — grew by **784** bytes: 407 of manifest, 30 of meta, 347 of
`registered.json`. The test asserts the remainder is smaller than the
*smallest* object the manifest declares (4096), so there is no room
under the figure for an object, and that no file anywhere under the root
is named for either object digest. The hub's `PackHolders` answer
carries **407** bytes — the manifest, verbatim — against 12288 declared.

**Each change removed once, and the detector watched to fire.** Three
removals in one build, each mapping to a distinct test: dropping the
`a_pack_may_be_registered` call, the `a_pack_may_be_fetched` call and
both `runner_gone` calls left exactly the three dependent tests red and
the other three green. A second build dropping the `registry.announced`
call (and, then, the `ReadPacksAgain` send) left exactly the other three
red. Removing the `JobDirStore` override of `packs()` — leaving the
default `None` in place — failed the seam test on the `Some` it expects,
4 passed 1 failed.

**And the suite itself caught a red this branch had introduced**, which
is the evidence that matters most here: the first full `./scripts/test.sh`
run was `FAILED (cargo test exited 101)` with seven named failures, and
the first command that ran it had piped its output to `tail`, so the
shell reported **exit 0** over a red suite. That is
`a-gate-joined-by-a-semicolon-is-advice` in a second costume — a pipe
discards the status exactly as `;` does — and the run was repeated with
the status preserved before anything was concluded from it.

## Not done, and why

- **Part three, the assignment side, is not begun.** `Asked.needs` and
  `Asked.leaves` ride the wire and `FromHub::AssignJob`'s `packs_in` /
  `packs_out` are still sent empty, so a job is assigned no pack and the
  resolve is only ever reached by a runner that asks. Stopped at a
  commit boundary with parts one and two proved, which the brief said to
  prefer. Resume point below.
- **D231 decision 5 is still owed, on both sides of the ask.** The hub
  does not send `ReadPacksAgain` (withdrawn on a measurement, above) and
  a runner neither answers it nor constructs `ToHub::PacksHeld`
  (`herd-runner/src/` was off limits). So an announcement only ever
  arrives unprompted and today's runner sends none. **What is proved is
  narrower than "the frames are wired": a hub that is told about a
  holding records it and forgets it when the link goes.** This is the
  one claim in this handoff most likely to be read as more than it is.
- **`AskTransferCapability` is refused, not served.** The hub's half of
  D209 — minting the token and pushing `ExpectTransfer` to the holder
  over the holder's own link — is unwritten, so "a capability pushed to
  the holder and to nobody else" is **not** proved and is not claimed.
  The frame is answered non-terminally by name instead of closing the
  link, which is a strict improvement on what it did before and not the
  feature.
- **`PackRegistry::forget_all_holdings` still has no production
  caller.** It is now the only part of the holdings mechanism without
  one, and its doc says why: a fresh registry starts empty and a
  rejoining runner's previous holding was already dropped by
  `runner_gone`, so what it is really for is a fleet-wide re-ask, which
  belongs with the runner half.
- **No commit-consistency check on a registration.** The hub does not
  compare `RegisterPack.commit` against the record's declared commit.
  Deliberate: the pack's commit is "the commit the pack was built at as
  the declaration resolved it", which need not equal the record's
  declaration commit once `ran_commit` (D176) differs, and getting that
  wrong would refuse good packs. Named as a finding, not left silent.

## Risks and findings

1. **Nobody asks and nobody answers.** The honest statement of what this
   branch achieves: a hub *told* about a holding records it and forgets
   it when the link goes, and no hub tells itself to ask and no runner
   volunteers. D231 decision 5 is not true. Everything is measured
   against a hand-driven peer; nothing against the real runner binary. A
   reader who takes "the frames are wired" to mean "holdings work in
   production" would be wrong. `registry.rs`'s module note now says this
   in the tree, replacing the note that said none of it was reachable —
   so the tree is not left claiming more than was done.
2. **A wrapping `JobStore` double silently loses the registry.**
   `server.rs`'s `AStoreWithAWindowForcedOpen` wraps a real
   `JobDirStore` and forwards 36 methods; it does not override `packs()`,
   so it answers `None` through the default body although the store
   inside it has a registry. Harmless today — no pack test uses it — and
   exactly the shape that bites later. The default body is still the
   right trade (it is what let the seam land without moving five impls),
   but a future pack test built on a wrapping double will measure the
   wrong hub.
3. **The brief named `herd-hub/src/store.rs` as "the trait seam"; the
   trait is in `herd-hub/src/job.rs`.** `pub trait JobStore` is at
   `job.rs:1961`; `store.rs` holds only the `impl` and the inherent
   method. So this branch edits `job.rs`, which the brief's write-path
   list did not name. Disclosed rather than worked around; D246's own
   evidence line already cites `herd-hub/src/job.rs:1961`, so the intent
   is not in doubt.
4. **`AskPack` carries no attempt number, so `is_current` is not merely
   the wrong window for a fetch — it is inconstructible.** The brief
   said both windows are "outside `is_current`"; for the fetch family it
   is stronger than that. `is_current` is answered out of
   `State::standing_of(id, attempt, speaker)` and the frame has no
   `attempt` field to supply. An agent who reached for `is_current` here
   would have had to invent an attempt number, which is how a frame about
   the wrong attempt gets through an ownership gate.
5. **`ToHub::PacksHeld` has no acknowledgement in the frame set**, so a
   refusal of it cannot go on the wire. A registry-less hub journals the
   refusal by name and leaves the link up; `FromHub::Refused` would
   close it (D13) and there is no non-terminal frame without a `job_id`.
   This is a gap in the *surface*, not in this wiring, and closing it
   would cost a protocol version — which is not a thing to decide at
   05:00, so it was not touched. `herd-proto/*` is byte-identical to
   `main` on this branch.
6. **A registration from a superseded attempt is refused, and that is a
   choice.** The pack may well be byte-identical — identical outputs
   give the identical pack — so the refusal costs a good pack
   occasionally. It is still right: accepting it risks a second id on one
   `(commit, key)`, which the registry then refuses to **every later
   job** for ever. The refusal's sentence says so.
7. **`announced()` keeps the endpoint from the last frame of a
   multi-frame announcement.** `PackRegistry::announced` sets
   `holding.endpoint` on every call including `more: true` ones. The hub
   passes the same `State::mesh_endpoint_of` each time within one link,
   so this cannot differ today. It is a latent surprise if an
   announcement ever spans a reconnection.
