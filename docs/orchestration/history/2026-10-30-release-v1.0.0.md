## Risks and findings

### 1. "42 of 44" is a scope figure, not a count of what is met

**This is the finding worth the most in this handoff, and it contradicts
the brief.** The brief's scope line reads *"42 of 44 of the first
consumer's musts"*. **`docs/coverage.md` does say 42 of 44 is the figure
to quote, and D248 records it — but neither says 42 musts are met.**
Read off the tree at `df47266`:

* `docs/coverage.md:21`-`:25`: **36 of 44** wholly on `main`; **4** part
  (E-12, E-16, E-17, E-37); **2** with none of it (E-14, E-42); **2**
  the consumer released us from (E-49, E-50). 36 + 4 + 2 + 2 = 44.
* `docs/coverage.md:41`-`:48`: *"the figure to quote is **42 of 44**"* —
  and its derivation is **44 minus the two waivers**, not a count of
  delivery. D205 claimed 44 of 44; D206 took E-49 and E-50 out; 42 is
  what is left **in scope**.
* **E-14's withdrawal, which the brief reports as new today, moves that
  arithmetic and nothing in the tree carries it yet.** E-14 is a must
  (`coverage.md:71`, `planned`, M12). Withdrawn, the released-from set
  is **three** and the in-scope figure is **41 of 44**, not 42.

**So "v1.0.0 ships 42 of 44 musts" cannot be published as written**
without breaking the one rule the brief gave the document. The body
therefore states the sourced arithmetic as a table — 36 wholly, 4 part,
1 (E-42) with nothing, 3 released from, summing to 44 — and says in the
next sentence that a placed must is not a delivered one. **Alexa's
choice, not mine:** he may prefer to publish "41 of 44 in scope" or
"42 of 44" as a headline beside that table. The table is what the tree
supports today, and whichever headline he signs, the two figures must
not be presented as the same quantity.

### 3. The migration refusal's mechanism, sharpened in the body

The brief says the consumer's *"3334 existing records are refused by
version, not degraded"*. True in effect; the **mechanism** is not the
per-record path. Between v0.4.1 and v1.0.0 **both** disk numbers move,
so the `LAYOUT_VERSION` check refuses the whole root **before any record
is read** and the hub does not start; the per-record quarantine of D218
and D219 never runs on this upgrade (`docs/operating.md:640`-`:644`).
An operator told to expect *"the hub starts and quarantines"* would
misread what they see. The body carries the layout refusal as the thing
that happens and the record behaviour as the thing that does not.

**And the control is a three-record fixture, not their root.** The
measurement quoted — 3 of 3 at version 1, 0 of 3 at version 2 — is
`herd-hub report` over a fixture
(`history/2026-10-30-merge-batch-11.md:325`-`:326`). Nobody in this
project has read the consumer's root (invariant 9, D161) and the body
does not imply otherwise.

The brief gives *"a few GB a year against **GiB a day** of outputs"*.

consumer's 14 days (`docs/disk-policy.md:152`, marked derived), and the

different quantity and I could not source it to a measurement, so it is
not in the body.

### 6. What the body claims about the gate going through Herd

The body says this release *"was built and gated through Herd itself"*,
on the evidence under *Gates*. **Read it narrowly:** our runner has no
delegated cgroup, so Herd **ordered** this work and did not **bound** it
(**D258**, last paragraph). The body does not claim otherwise, and its
third operator limit says the same thing about any host without
delegation.

### 7. `release.yml` does not read the human half for correctness

`check-docs.sh` requires the file to **exist and be non-empty** for the
workspace version. Nothing checks what it says. So a wrong sentence in
`deploy/release-notes/1.0.0.md` reaches the published page with no gate
in front of it, which is exactly why the body is Alexa's act (**D210**)
and why this handoff states every figure's source.

### 8. My own first draft named the wrong instrument for a figure

Found by re-reading the committed body, and fixed in `4422bd7`. The
retention section attributed **both** of the consumer's stock figures to
`herd-hub report`. Only one is: **105.57 GiB** of collected files is
`report`'s (`docs/disk-policy.md` R14), and the **≈ 346 MiB** of
records, logs and event files is their own `du -sk` (R16). The same
paragraph also carried *"a few gigabytes a year"*, which nothing
measured — it was a division I did, not a reading anybody took.
Replaced by the measured ratio, **0.32 % against 99.68 %**.

**Recorded rather than quietly fixed**, because it is the exact failure
the document's own rule exists to prevent and it got past one pass of my
own sourcing. A reader checking that figure against `disk-policy.md`
would have found the number right and the instrument wrong, which is
harder to catch than a wrong number.
