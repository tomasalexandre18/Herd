## What changed and why

No milestone moves and **no `E-nn` State cell changes**: the must tally
is re-derived mechanically below and does not move. What moves is the
**truthfulness of the record** and two runner defects the first
consumer's own job records exposed.

`main` can now say three things it could not. A **requester** sees what
its job took and which cap stopped it, in words, with `rc 124` still one
code for all four caps. A **runner** charges one OOM kill to the one
step it happened in, instead of charging a single kernel event to two
steps and then overriding a successful takedown's exit code with a cap
verdict. And the **record** no longer claims that the kernel enforces
each job's declared memory figure: it enforces one runner-wide ceiling,
the same figure for every job, which is a different promise.

Behind the last one sits the measurement that makes `E-29` and `E-31`
not independent: a job's tmpfs bytes are charged to its own
`memory.max`. That is a **finding about what Herd promises**, sized as
ORCH-312 and not fixed here.

## Risks and findings

1. **The gate is not a deterministic function of the sha on a busy
   host, and this landing is the first to have to plan around it.**
   ORCH-321's test reddens whatever branch holds the gate when load is
   high. The mitigation used here was timing, not a code change:
   `./scripts/bench-preflight.sh` read by its **verdict line** and not
   by `$?` through a pipe, plus `/proc/loadavg` read directly, aiming
   near or below load1 **6** rather than at the preflight's own 9.00
   ceiling. **This is a method hazard, not a one-off**, and until
   ORCH-321 is closed every batch pays it.
2. **A drafted `decisions.md` entry can be over 79 columns and no
   branch gate will catch it**, because the draft lives inside a fenced
   block in the handoff and `check-docs.sh` exempts fenced code. D282's
   header was 82 columns and was caught only by the merger's own
   `check-docs.sh` after appending. Worth a rule: the merger always runs
   `check-docs.sh` **after** allocating, which this landing did.
3. **D280's tally paragraph was stale on arrival**, giving 1 `planned`
   and 1 `withdrawn` where the tree gives 0 and 2. Found only because
   the brief asked for a mechanical re-derivation and the figures
   disagreed. **The tree was checked rather than the number patched**,
   and the headline figures were never wrong. Filed as **ORCH-322**.
4. **The brief attributed a decision to the wrong entry, and nothing was
   lost.** It expected `herdctl-peak-and-cap-legibility`'s entry to
   carry *"per-job ceilings and packing by declaration after
   measurement"*; that decision is recorded in **D283**, `shmem-charge`'s
   entry, while **D282**'s own two decisions are the peak's visibility
   and `rc 124` staying one code. Both are in the batch, verbatim as
   drafted. Nothing was recomposed to match the brief.
5. **`ORCH-316`'s *Depends on* cell referenced a provisional id and was
   not in the brief's list of three.** Renumbering only what a brief
   names is not safe; the file has to be swept and each hit judged.
6. **Two of the `ORCH-NEXT` hits in `board.md` are historical and must
   never be renumbered** — ORCH-243 and ORCH-237 describe *earlier*
   batches' placeholders, including the batch-15 collision that
   ORCH-237 exists to record. A blind sweep falsifies them.
7. **`oom-step-attribution` lands without a green gate of its own.**
   Its `.rs` was last judged by `j0000000179`, which was red on a test
   it cannot reach. This landing's own gate on the merged tree is the
   only green evidence its code has, which is exactly what the method
   intends — a green gate does not travel between worktrees — but a
