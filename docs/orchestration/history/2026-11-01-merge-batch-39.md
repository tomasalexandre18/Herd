## What changed and why

**A release, not a milestone.** `main` gains no capability at this landing:
every line of Rust in it was already on `main` at `40f21ca`. What it gains is
a **version** — `Cargo.toml` and the four `Cargo.lock` members move from
1.0.0 to 1.1.0 — and the human half `release.yml` reads,
`deploy/release-notes/1.1.0.md`, 332 lines, in the tree where the workflow
finds it with no paste (ORCH-162). Alexa validated the publication on
2026-11-01 and chose the number.

What `main` can say now that it could not before: **the first release on the
far side of v1.0.0's one reset**. `PROTOCOL_VERSION` 3, `RECORD_VERSION` 2 and
`LAYOUT_VERSION` 2 are the values at `v1.0.0` and at this landing, read at both
refs through `release.yml`'s own three `sed` expressions, so **no state root is
reset, no migration is owed and `docs/operating.md`'s four steps do not apply
to this upgrade**. The release carries the two halves of the consumer's
2026-10-31 incident (**D291**, **D292**, **D294**) and states that the second
is **bounded, not removed**. The upgrade order is **every runner, then the
hub, required and not advisory**: a v1.0.0 runner sends no
`still_running_attempt`, the hub reads an absent field as an absence of
testimony, and a hub upgraded alone fixes nothing.

It also **names a promise it does not keep**. v1.0.0's published page says
record retention is *"cut from v1.0.0, following in v1.1"*; this is v1.1.0 and
the engine does not exist. Alexa chose to publish naming the gap, over holding
the tag and over a number that does not claim 1.1, so that section of the body
is his answer and not a draft — **nobody softens it later on the ground that
it reads harshly**, and the *mixed fleet* section stays labelled **unmeasured**
with no figure in it.

No milestone row moves and no `E-nn` expectation changes state: a release is
not a measurement.
